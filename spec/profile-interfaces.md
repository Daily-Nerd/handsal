# handsal profile, version 1, part 2

This file is the second part of the normative profile. `spec/profile.md` holds sections 0 to 7 (reading guide, scope, definitions, act profile, key classes, genesis and pin, fold, verifier rules). This file holds sections 8 and 9 (rendering, conformance and checker CLI). `spec/profile-part3.md` holds sections 10 to 14 (signer CLI, reason codes, vector groups, freshness witness, known limitations) and the appendices. Section numbers and requirement ids are global to the profile: a reference such as "section 9" or `KC-02` names one place whichever file it is read in. The three files are one text, hashed as three core files (CF-01).

## 8. Rendering

### RN-01 Pure function

The render, `render({attachments, kid, method, payload}) -> {first_line, text}`, is a pure function: byte-for-byte deterministic, ASCII only, with no locale, no clock and no I/O. Lines end with LF, with no trailing blank line and one final LF. The signer's `render` command is that function composed with the signer's own fill (SG-04): it takes `{attachments, draft}`, fills `performer_id` and `kid` from its key and `ts` from its clock as `sign` does, and so needs a key (`key_unavailable`, exit 3, without one) and shows no prompt. Given a `payload` and a `kid` instead of a draft, the function is the CF-03 `render`, which a signer runs only as `handsal-sign-conformance render` (CF-04). Attachments are listed in RN-09.

Why: the render is a pure function of signed bytes plus bound attachments, in ASCII, so independent implementations need no Unicode tables.

- S1: GIVEN the same input twice WHEN rendered THEN equal bytes. V: rnd/determinism

### RN-02 Layout

Line 1 is the first line (RN-03). Then, in order: `Method: <method>`, `Org: <hash12(org)>`, `Signer: <performer_id> key <kid12>`, the method lines (RN-04), `Signed at: <ts>`, `Expires: <expires>` when present, `Parent: <hash12(parent_receipt_hash)>` when non-null, and `Head: <hash12(head.id)>` when present. Verdicts add `Sender: not proven` (RN-07). The org name is shown only for `genesis.sign`, from the signed document: for other acts the signer does not fold, so a name could only come from the caller, and the caller never chooses the words.

Every line after line 1 is at most 100 bytes. A longer line is written as its first 98 bytes, with no prefix, and then continuation lines, each `+ ` followed by the next at most 98 bytes, so no signed byte is hidden; a 253-character relay hostname stays fully visible. A render longer than 72 lines is refused (`render_refused`).

- S1: GIVEN an approve payload WHEN rendered THEN the text matches the anchor. V: rnd/approve, rnd/reject, rnd/no-org-name-non-genesis, rnd/long-line-wrapped, rnd/render-73-lines-refused

### RN-03 First line

Line 1 is at most 80 bytes, is the OS prompt text (RN-11), and MUST carry the decision summary. A free field longer than 12 characters appears as its first 11 characters plus `~`. The free fields are `<class>`, `<person>`, `<member>`, `<role>`, `<request_id>`, the decision `<consumer>` and `<subject>`, and the genesis `<name>` (`org_name`, up to 24 characters, so it is cut like the others). `kid12`, `hash12` and decimal counts appear as is. Templates:
- `device.add`: `Add device <kid12> (<class>) to <person>`
- `device.remove`: `Remove device <kid12> of <person>`
- `member.add`: `Add device <kid12> as member <member> (<role>)`
- `chain.resolve`: `Resolve fork, keep: ` followed by line 1 of the chosen candidate cut to 59 bytes, plus `~` when cut
- `request.approve`: `APPROVE request <request_id> (<hash12(inputs_hash)>, <N> bytes)`; `request.reject`: `REJECT request ...`; `request.needs-info`: `NEEDS-INFO on request ...`, with the same tail
- `decision`: `<consumer>: <action> <subject> (<hash12(inputs_hash)>, <N> bytes)`. `<action>` is shown whole, never cut: its grammar bounds it at 24 characters (section 2.2), so a consumer and a subject cut to 12 characters each, the hash12 and a size of four digits (the content block bound is 2048 bytes, RN-05) give a line 1 of at most 80 bytes
- `genesis.sign` over an original: `Sign genesis of <name> (<hash12(doc)>): <M> members, <D> devices`
- `genesis.sign` over a change: `Change <name> (<hash12(doc)>): +<A>/-<R> dev, <C> mem, policy same|NEW`, where A and R are the devices added and removed, C counts member entries added, removed or with a changed role, and `NEW` means some policy byte differs
- `act.endorse`: `Endorse: ` followed by line 1 of the endorsed act cut to 70 bytes, plus `~` when cut

There is one vector per method at maximum field lengths proving the 80-byte bound.

### RN-04 Method lines

Every signed value a person decides on is rendered: one line per field, in ACT-09 order, values verbatim. A field line is the field name, a colon, one space and the value (`Person: alice`). A list is a header line, the name and a colon with nothing after it, followed by one indented line per entry. `hash12` and the thumbprint of a key are defined in section 2.1. The lines, in order:
- `device.add`: `Person: <person>`, `New device: <full kid>`, `Agree key: <thumbprint of agree>`, `Class: <class>` (on its own line, so endorsers vouch for it), `Label: <label>`, `Link code: <hash12(link_code_hash)>`.
- `device.remove`: `Person: <person>`, `Remove device: <full kid>`.
- `member.add`: `Member: <member>`, `Role: <role>`, `New device: <full kid>`, `Agree key: <thumbprint of agree>`, `Class: <class>`, `Label: <label>`.
- `chain.resolve`: `Fork parent: <full id>`, `Choose: <full id>`; `Candidates:` and one line per candidate in `candidates` order, `  > <hash12> <line 1>` for the chosen one and `    <hash12> <line 1>` for the others; `Heads:` and one line per head in `heads` order, `  - <hash12> <line 1 of the attached head act>`; then `Overrides: none`, or `Overrides:` and one line per entry in entry order, `  - <hash12 of resolve> (endorsements: <E>)`, E the decimal count.
- `request.*`: `Request: <request_id>`, `Recipient: <recipient>`, `Relay: <relay>`, `Request hash: <hash12(inputs_hash)>`, then the request block (RN-05).
- `decision`: `Consumer: <consumer>`, `Action: <action>`, `Subject: <subject>`, `Decision hash: <hash12(inputs_hash)>`, then the decision block (RN-12). A decision has no `Sender` line: it names no sender and no recipient.
- `genesis.sign` over an original: `Org name: <name>`, `Profile: <full profile hash>`, `Members:` and per member `  - <id> <role>`, `Devices:` and per device four lines, `  - <full kid> of <person>`, `    Class: <class>`, `    Label: <label>`, `    Agree: <thumbprint of agree>`, then the policy lines (below), then `Document hash: <hash12 of the document hash>`. Members and devices are in document order, which is sorted (GP-01). A change renders as a difference (RN-10).
- `act.endorse`: `Endorsed act: <full receipt id>`, then the endorsed act's method lines. For an endorsed resolve these run from its first method line through its last `Overrides` line, never the resolve's line 1, `Method`, `Org`, `Signer`, `Signed at` or `Parent`, so the endorser sees the same method lines as the resolve's signer.

The policy lines are the same in an original and in a change: `Policy:`, `  Roles: <roles joined by ",">` in document order, one line per rule in ascending order of rule name, `  Rule <name>: <persons> of <roles joined by ",">`, one line per entry of `classes` in document order, `  Class <class>: <user_verification>`, `  Clock skew: <clock_skew_s> s`, `  Max validity: <max_validity_s> s`, `  Max view age: <max_view_age_s> s`, `  Relays: none` or `  Relays: <hosts joined by ",">` in document order, and the witness: `  Witness: none`, or `  Witness: relay`, `  Relay key: <full relay_kid>`, `  Max tip age: <max_tip_age_s> s` (WT-08).

Entries inside a change section keep document order (RN-10): the added members, the changed roles and the added devices in the order of the new document, the removed members and devices in the order of the previous one. Both documents are sorted (GP-01), so members run by `id` and devices by `kid`.

Not rendered: the envelope constants (fixed by ACT-01) and `nonce` (signer-chosen randomness with no decision value).

At the ACT-09 caps a resolve renders at most 60 lines (line 1, 3 header lines, 54 method lines, `Signed at`, `Parent`), each under 100 bytes, inside the 72-line limit. Its endorsement is exactly 62 lines (line 1, `Method`, `Org`, `Signer`, `Endorsed act`, the 54 method lines, `Signed at`, `Parent`, `Head`). The longest `Overrides` entry line is 37 bytes.

- S1: GIVEN a genesis change WHEN rendered THEN policy changes are visible as lines. V: rnd/genesis-policy-lines, rnd/genesis-class-own-line, wit/render-genesis-witness-relay
- S2: GIVEN a resolve and its endorsement at the caps WHEN rendered by the checker and by each signer THEN the exact anchored text. V: rnd/resolve-max-caps-fits, rnd/endorse-resolve-max-caps-fits
- S3: GIVEN a resolve with one override entry of one endorsement WHEN rendered THEN the line `  - <hash12> (endorsements: 1)`. V: rnd/resolve-override-entry-line, rnd/resolve-override-entry-line-one
- S4: GIVEN a `device.add` and a genesis document WHEN rendered THEN the agree key and the full profile hash appear. V: rnd/device-add-agree-key-id, rnd/genesis-agree-key-id, rnd/genesis-profile-full, rnd/endorse-shows-endorsed-id, rnd/resolve-candidates-listed, rnd/resolve-heads-attached
- S5: GIVEN a policy whose `relays` is `["r.example"]` WHEN an original is rendered THEN the line `  Relays: r.example` follows `  Max view age`. V: rnd/genesis-relays-line

### RN-05 Request block

Request bytes are untrusted and are not decoded as UTF-8. The block starts with `----- request (<N> bytes, sha256 <hash12(inputs_hash)>) -----` and ends with `----- end -----`. Each source line (bytes up to an LF) is rendered as `| ` plus its escaped bytes: 0x20 to 0x7E as is, and every other byte (CR, TAB, NUL, DEL, every byte of a multi-byte UTF-8 sequence, bidirectional controls) as `\xHH` in lowercase. Escaped content longer than 96 bytes continues on lines that are `|+` (no space) followed by the next at most 96 bytes, never splitting an escape. An empty source line is `| ` (a pipe and a space). A final LF ends the last line and does not start another, so an empty request has no content line.

There is no truncation. A request whose block would exceed 2048 source bytes or 32 block lines is refused (`render_refused`) by every renderer, so a signer never signs bytes the person could not see. The 32 lines are the content lines, continuations included, without the two delimiter lines.

Why: no Unicode tables (they differ across implementation languages and versions); every content line starts with `|`, so request text cannot spoof the end delimiter; bidirectional and control bytes are visible. The cost is that non-ASCII text shows as escapes, for example `\xc3\xa1` for an accented letter.

- S1: GIVEN request text containing a line `----- end -----` WHEN rendered THEN it appears as `| ----- end -----` and does not end the block. V: rnd/hostile-delimiter, rnd/hostile-bidi
- S2: GIVEN a request over 2048 bytes, or of 33 or 40 block lines WHEN rendered THEN `render_refused`; GIVEN exactly 2048 bytes THEN rendered. V: rnd/request-over-2048-refused, rnd/request-33-lines-refused, rnd/oversize-truncated, rnd/request-32-lines-cap. C: rnd/request-exactly-2048-ok
- S3: GIVEN a request of zero bytes, or one line of 300 bytes WHEN rendered THEN the block has no content line, or a `| ` line followed by `|+` lines. V: rnd/empty-request, rnd/request-long-line-continuation

### RN-09 Attachments are bound to the payload

Every renderer (checker `render`, signer `render` and `sign`) MUST verify every attachment against the payload before rendering:

| Method | Attachment | Binding |
|---|---|---|
| verdicts | `request_b64` | hashstr(bytes) = `inputs_hash` |
| `decision` | `decision_b64` | hashstr(bytes) = `inputs_hash` |
| `genesis.sign` | `document` | hashstr(JCS(document)) = `inputs_hash` |
| `genesis.sign` over a change | `previous` | hashstr(JCS(previous)) = `document.prev` |
| `genesis.sign` over a change | `document` | `head.id` of the payload = `document.head` |
| `act.endorse` | `act` (JWS) | its receipt id = `body.act` = `parent_receipt_hash`; its `performer_id` = `parent_performer_id`; its `parent_receipt_hash` = `head.id`; it is a chain primary or a resolve |
| `act.endorse` of a resolve | `candidates`, `heads` (JWS lists) | bound against the attached resolve exactly as for the resolve itself (next rows) |
| `chain.resolve` | `candidates` (JWS list) | one JWS per id of `body.candidates`; each has `parent_receipt_hash` = the resolve's parent |
| `chain.resolve` | `heads` (JWS list) | one JWS per id of `body.heads`; each is a chain primary |

Every attachment that is an act MUST pass ACT-01 to ACT-14. An attachment that is a genesis document (`document`, `previous`) MUST be valid by itself: GP-15 steps 1 and 3 over the document alone (structure, then policy and devices; the profile and lineage steps belong to the fold). `fixture_key` follows the profile the document itself names: a document naming the production profile that lists a fixture kid is not valid alone, and one naming the fixture profile, or a profile the checker does not implement (step 2 belongs to the fold), is not refused for it. One that binds by hash but is not a valid document is `attachment_mismatch`, the same code as an act attachment that fails ACT-01 to ACT-14. A JWS list bound to a body list holds exactly one JWS per receipt id of that list and nothing else, in any order: a duplicate copy, an extra JWS or a missing id is `attachment_mismatch`. An `act.endorse` of a chain primary that carries `candidates` or `heads` is `attachment_mismatch`. A name that is absent from `attachments` is `attachment_missing`. Every other defect of an attachment is `attachment_mismatch`: a list that is present but is not an array, a value of the wrong type (`request_b64` and `decision_b64` excepted, CF-03), a list that lacks an id, or an attachment that does not bind. Whether a head lies in a losing subtree is decided by the fold (FO-25), not by the renderer. A signer MUST NOT sign what its renderer refuses.

Order of checks. A renderer decides in this order and the first defect wins: the envelope and the method; for `genesis.sign` the `document` (absent, not bound, or not valid alone), because it fixes whether a head is due; the form of the act object and body, including the presence of the `head`; then the other attachments in the order of the table above, each read and bound completely (present, of the right type, bound) before the next is looked at. An `act.endorse` is decided on `act` first, and only an attached resolve then asks for `candidates` and then `heads`: an endorse whose attached act does not bind is `attachment_mismatch` whether or not `heads` is there, and so is a `chain.resolve` whose `candidates` do not bind, whether or not `heads` is there. For `genesis.sign`, `previous` is read after the head presence is checked, then `head.id` against `document.head`. The signer's own comparisons of a draft with an attachment (SG-08, SG-13) take their places in this order and add none of their own.

Why: the words a person sees are a function of signed bytes only; an attachment is never a second, unbound source.

### RN-10 Genesis change rendering and the size bound

A change renders as a difference from the previous document (attachment `previous`, RN-09). Its method lines, in order: `Org name: <name>`, `Profile: <full profile hash>` (the two lines an original opens with, so a rename or a profile change is never hidden), `Previous: <hash12(prev)>`, `Head: <full H>`, then these sections, each written only when it has an entry: `Members added:` and `Members removed:` (per member `  - <id> <role>`), `Role changed:` (`  - <id> <old role> -> <new role>`), `Devices added:` (the four device lines of RN-04 per device), `Devices removed:` (per device `  - <full kid> of <person>`); then the policy lines of RN-04 in full, and last `Unchanged: <D> devices, <M> members`, D the devices in both documents and M the members in both documents with the same role. A change has no `Document hash` line: hash12 of the document is in line 1 (RN-03). `Head` also appears as hash12 in the RN-02 frame; both come from `head.id`, which equals `document.head` (RN-09). Carried devices are byte-identical (GP-20), so there is no "devices changed" section. An original renders in full (RN-08).

Fold bound: a genesis document whose `genesis.sign` render exceeds 72 lines is `genesis_render_too_large` (GP-15 step 5). The fold computes it with the same render function, which is the single owner of "fits". The count does not depend on the signer: `Org`, `Signer`, `Signed at` and `Head` are one line each whoever signs, so the fold counts the render with any performer id, kid and time.

Why: every valid genesis document is signable by every listed device. An organization grows through chain acts, and each change shows only what changes, so the 72-line cap does not block growth. A difference with a fold bound is preferred over paging, which would split one decision across screens of an untrusted channel.

- S1: GIVEN a change WHEN rendered THEN the difference, the head line and the full policy appear. V: rnd/genesis-change-diff, rnd/genesis-change-head-line, rnd/genesis-sign-change
- S2: GIVEN a change that renames the organization WHEN rendered THEN `Org name` and `Profile` open the method lines. V: rnd/genesis-change-org-name-profile-lines
- S3: GIVEN a change that adds a relay host WHEN rendered THEN the full policy shows the new `Relays` line. V: rnd/genesis-change-relays-diff

### RN-11 The display is not a trust boundary

The signer's display channel (`/dev/tty`, `CONOUT$`) and the OS prompt text are both reachable by same-user processes (section 1.2). The full render and line 1 are aids. The signer MUST still put line 1 into the OS prompt; where a platform prompt cannot carry it, that is stated for that class. Chain security comes from FO-22. A tricked touch on a non-chain act signs one decision whose request, or consumer, action and subject, hash12 and size are in line 1; a consumer that needs more requires decisions from more than one recipient, which is consumer policy outside this profile. The ceremony's hash12 comparison is an aid (SG-12).

### RN-12 Decision block

`decision` renders its attached content exactly like a request block (RN-05 escaping, prefixes and limits), delimited by `----- decision (<N> bytes, sha256 <hash12>) -----` and `----- end -----`, from the bound attachment `decision_b64` (RN-09). Over the limits it is `render_refused`, so a signer never signs content the person could not see. A content of zero bytes has no content line and line 1 says `0 bytes`: the decision is then about its `subject` alone, and the person sees that.

### CF-01 Profile hashes and bundle

- **Production profile** = hashstr(JCS({"core": <core manifest>, "kind": "production"})). **Fixture profile** = hashstr(JCS({"core": <core manifest>, "kind": "fixture"})) (GP-18).
- **Bundle hash** = hashstr(JCS(full manifest: the core plus every vector file)), published with each tag for the integrity of the vector set.

### CF-02 Vector format

Vector files follow the vitni format: one JSON file per vector, `{name, command, input | input_raw, anchor}`. Unlike vitni, `anchor` is REQUIRED on every handsal vector: vitni can run without anchors because two independent verifiers cross-check each other, while handsal starts with one reference. `input_raw` MAY carry bytes that JSON would normalize. Names are `group/case`. Vectors over MAX_INPUT are built by the harness at run time from their row, a valid input padded inside one string to MAX_INPUT + 1 bytes; no 16 MiB file is stored.

- S1: GIVEN a vector without `anchor` WHEN the harness loads it THEN it fails. T: conformance/tests/test_run.py::test_vector_without_anchor_is_rejected

`pin` and `now` are inputs of the conformance build only. The production build (CF-08) takes neither: it reads the pin through the platform probe and `now` from the system clock. For `check-act`, `recheck-act`, `enrolled` and `head` it reads `acts` and `lineage` from the store (CF-09), and `check-act`, `enrolled` and `head` also its `synced_at` and `next` (`recheck-act` checks no view age and no `next`, VR-17), and they take no `tip_statement`; an input carrying `pin`, `now`, `acts`, `lineage`, `next`, `synced_at` or `tip_statement` is `malformed_input` for those commands. `recheck-act` takes no `now` and no `next` in either build and reads no clock; the production build reads only the pin and the store (VR-17). `admit`, `pool` and `compact` read no pin, no clock and no store: their whole input is the snapshot of CF-13 and the item or the key, or the snapshot alone (CF-11, CF-12, CF-14).

`render` takes exactly the keys `attachments`, `kid`, `method` and `payload`, else `malformed_input`. `payload` is the signed payload as a JSON object (not its bytes and not its base64url text), `kid` is the `kid` of the JWS header and `method` the method string to render, both strings, and `attachments` is an object. The names RN-09 binds are the only ones read: `request_b64` and `decision_b64` (strings, standard base64 with padding, section 2.2), `document` and `previous` (genesis documents as JSON objects), `act` (a compact JWS string), and `candidates` and `heads` (arrays of compact JWS strings). A `request_b64` or `decision_b64` that is not a string or not canonical base64 is `malformed_input`; any other attachment of the wrong JSON type is `attachment_mismatch`. Any other name is ignored.

`genesis-check` validates every link of `lineage` in order from the original: GP-03 for the original, GP-19 for each change against the path to its H in the epoch before it, and the GP-15 order per document. It returns the first failure, so `valid:true` means every link is authorized (GP-21). The ceremony and the pin helper use it.

Command inputs follow CF-08 and CF-09.

`kid` takes exactly `{x, y}`, two strings of base64url characters, and returns the thumbprint of those strings as given. It checks neither length nor curve: whether the key is valid is the concern of the act that introduces it (ACT-11). Any other shape is `malformed_input`.

Input framing: stdin MUST be I-JSON (RFC 7493: UTF-8, no duplicate member names, no lone surrogates) of at most MAX_INPUT bytes. Size is checked first, before parsing. A duplicate member name at any depth, invalid UTF-8, or more than MAX_INPUT bytes is `{"error":"unparseable_input"}`, before any command logic. Nesting deeper than MAX_NESTING is `unparseable_input` too: arrays and objects count together, the root value counts as 1, and input of exactly MAX_NESTING levels passes the reader and meets the command's own checks. The bound is one number for every implementation, because a reader with a bound of its own answers differently at the edge, and a recursive reader with none lets hostile input exhaust the stack. Exception: `subset` reads its stdin as raw bytes (the value under test; more than MAX_INPUT is `unparseable_input`) and hands them to the recognizer unparsed.

Errors, all with exit 0: an unknown command is `{"error":"unsupported_command"}`; input that is not I-JSON within the bound is `{"error":"unparseable_input"}`; JSON missing or mistyping a required input is `{"error":"malformed_input"}`. A nonzero exit happens only for a crash, which consumers MUST treat as failure. Nothing but the result line goes to stdout. Output is deterministic. There is no checker `build` or `emit` command: emitted bytes are produced by signers (`handsal-sign emit`, SG-01) and anchored by the vector generator.

### CF-04 Implementations

Every checker implementation MUST produce byte-identical stdout for every vector. The harness (`conformance/run.py`, standard library only, `--impl "<argv>"` given one or more times) reports PASS, DIVERGE or ANCHOR-FAIL per vector, in the manner of vitni's `compare.mjs`. The checker role runs every command but `emit`, `recheck-act` included, and it is read from the vectors, not from a list in the harness.

A signer implementation runs the `render` and `emit` vectors, and only those, through its own conformance executable, `handsal-sign-conformance`. It is built from the same core as the shipped `handsal-sign` (SG-01), is never installed at an administrator path (SG-14, SG-15) and is not part of the signer's distribution. It takes one argument. `render` takes the CF-03 `render` input, read as CF-03 reads it, MAX_NESTING included, and writes the CF-03 output, with exit 0 for every result, so the `render` vectors are the ones the checker runs. `emit` is the shipped `emit` (SG-01, SG-04), with the same input, output and exit codes; a vector whose expected exit code is not 0 names it in its `exit` member. Any other argument is `{"error":"unsupported_command"}`, exit 2 (SG-08). The executable holds no key, shows no prompt and reads neither the clock nor a random source: every value its output depends on is in its input. `agree` is not a conformance command: its output depends on a private key, which this executable never holds, so its known answers are a file of public fixture keys (`conformance/fixtures/agree-kat.json`, SG-16) that each signer's own suite runs.

A signer has no `fold` and no `genesis-check`, so the `pol/*` rows are run by each signer's own suite: it reads the vector files and gives the first document of every one-document row to its check of a document alone (GP-15 steps 1 to 4), which must answer the row's code. The expectation is kept nowhere but in the vector, so the checker and the signers answer one set of rows.

Why: the shipped `render` takes a draft and fills `performer_id`, `kid` and `ts` from its own key and clock (SG-04, RN-01), so it can never take a `payload` and a `kid` from its caller, and a flag in it would be a way to make the signer show words for bytes it did not derive. As in CF-08, the split is a build, not a flag.

- S1: GIVEN a divergent implementation WHEN the harness runs THEN it reports DIVERGE. T: conformance/tests/test_run.py::test_diverge
- S2: GIVEN a `render` vector WHEN run through `handsal-sign-conformance` THEN the output the checker gives. V: rnd/approve, rnd/render-73-lines-refused
- S3: GIVEN an approve draft with fixed values WHEN run through `handsal-sign-conformance emit` THEN `{payload_hex}`; GIVEN a draft with a JSON number THEN exit 2, `schema_refused`. V: jcs/emit-value-with-number-refused. C: cli/build-approve
- S4: GIVEN a one-document `pol/*` row WHEN a signer's own suite runs its document through the check of a document alone THEN the row's code. T: signer/windows/tests/vectors.rs::pol_rows_through_check_alone, signer/apple/Tests/HandsalCoreTests/VectorRunnerTests.swift::polRowsThroughCheckAlone

### CF-05 Reference checker

The reference checker is Python (standard library logic) delegating ES256 to vitni behind a `Verifier` interface. The vitni binary is located only by an absolute administrator-owned path fixed per platform (SG-14): on macOS `/Library/Application Support/handsal/bin/vitni-verify`, checked like the pin (GP-10 `lstat` chain, root-owned, not group or other writable) before each spawn; on Linux, in the server build only (CF-10), `/opt/handsal/bin/vitni-verify`, checked the same way; on Windows `C:\Program Files\handsal\vitni-verify.exe`. There is no PATH lookup and no environment override; tests inject a path through a constructor argument only (the GP-14 pattern). A spawn error, an ownership failure, a timeout, non-JSON output or an `{"error"}` result is `verifier_unavailable`, never a skip, from `check-act`, `fold`, `enrolled` and `genesis-check`, which verify signatures through the same interface: each returns it in its own output form (the reason beside `valid:false`, `{"error"}`, or the `reason` of `enrolled` and `genesis-check`).

### CF-06 Vector generation

ES256 vectors use the fixed P-256 scalars of `conformance/fixtures/keys.json` (KC-07), RFC 6979 nonces and then low-S normalization, so every run is byte-identical. A rerun followed by `git diff --exit-code` over the vector directory is the regeneration check. The generator calls both vitni implementations (Go and TypeScript) on every positive JWS and aborts unless both say `ok`; negative signature vectors record vitni's verdict. Vectors cover the low-S and high-S forms of one payload; they are the ES256 coverage vitni lacks. Vector anchors are authored by the generator from the expected behaviour, never by running the checker under test. PRODK vectors are not regenerated (CF-08).

- S1: GIVEN the generator run twice WHEN outputs are compared THEN identical. T: conformance/vectors/_gen/tests/test_generate.py::test_deterministic_stub_is_reproducible

### CF-08 Checker builds: production, conformance, server

The checker ships as three builds of one pure core:
- **production** `handsal-check`, the only build installed at the administrator path (SG-14): `check-act`, `enrolled`, `head` and `fold` call the platform probe in-process at the fixed pin path and read `now` from the system clock. An input carrying `pin` or `now` is `malformed_input`. A lineage under the fixture profile is `fixture_profile`. `check-act`, `recheck-act`, `enrolled` and `head` also read the store (CF-09) in process, once per call, and take no `acts`, `lineage`, `next`, `synced_at` or `tip_statement`; `fold` and `genesis-check` take their bag explicitly and read no store. Every production command that reads chain state gets it from the one read of the store and the one pinned fold over it, so two commands never see two stores across a `sync`; a new per-machine query is a new command over them, never a second reader. `sync` exists in this build only.
- **conformance** `handsal-check-conformance`: the same commands with `pin`, `now` and the store's content (`acts`, `lineage`, `next`, `synced_at`, `tip_statement`) as input data, plus `admit`, `pool` and `compact`, and no `sync`. It implements both profiles and is never installed at an administrator path. Every vector runs against it (CF-04). No vector runs against the production build: it takes no `pin` and no `now`, so a vector input cannot set it up. Production-only behaviour is proven by implementation tests (CF-07).
- **server** `handsal-check-server` (CF-10): only `admit`, `pool` and `compact`, no pin, no clock, no store, no fixture profile. It is installed on automation hosts, never at a macOS or Windows administrator path, and its answers prove nothing about any person.

Production-profile vectors use PRODK: production keys generated once per tag, used to sign the committed production vectors, with their private scalars destroyed afterwards and not listed in `fixture-kids.json`. Their bytes are committed and covered by the bundle hash; they are not regenerated. Continuous integration injects the vitni path through the conformance build's constructor.

An owner-signed vector names one production profile. Every genesis document of its lineage carries the production profile hash, and an edit to any core file moves that hash (CF-01), so a signed set belongs to the core it names and to no later one. After such an edit the committed set is stale: it is removed from the tree in the same change (it stays in the tag that shipped it), its ids are pending again, and the next tag needs a new signing run with new keys. The catalog check reads the current production profile from `conformance/pins.json` and lists every owner-signed file that names another as `owner-stale`, so a stale set cannot sit in the tree unnoticed; at the tag, `--final` lists each pending id that has no file.

Why: a consumer at the administrator path can never be handed a forged probe, a forged clock or a fixture profile, a server never holds a command that proves a person, and every rule stays testable under both profiles. The build split enforces this, not a flag.

### CF-09 The per-machine store and `sync`

The production build keeps one store per organization and user: the view of the chain that every consumer on the machine is checked against, the retained acts of the chain (CF-14), the lineage, `next` and `synced_at`. Only `handsal-check sync` writes it. `check-act`, `recheck-act`, `enrolled` and `head` read it in process (CF-08) and take no chain state as input (VR-01, VR-17, GP-13, VR-19): a consumer passes the act, `seen` and the values it expects. The conformance build has no store and no `sync`: its inputs stay explicit (CF-04), so no vector changes.

Location. macOS: `<home>/Library/Application Support/handsal-check/<hex64>.store`, `hex64` as in GP-05 and `<home>` the home directory of the process's real uid from the directory service, never `$HOME` or any other environment variable. The directory is mode 0700 and the file 0600, both owned by that uid. The lock `<hex64>.lock` and the temporary file `<hex64>.tmp` live in the same directory. Windows: `<LocalAppData>\handsal-check\<hex64>.store`, `<LocalAppData>` the known folder of the process token (`FOLDERID_LocalAppData`), never `%LOCALAPPDATA%`. The Windows-side checker (GP-17 item 2) owns that store. A WSL2 consumer runs `/mnt/c/Program Files/handsal/handsal-check sync` and pipes acts on stdin; the WSL2-side checker never writes a store, because its probe is `pin_probe_unprotected` and `sync` checks the pin before it touches a path. Until the Windows gate exists (GP-11) Windows and WSL2 have no store.

Format `handsal-store/1`: one line of JCS and one LF, `{"acts":[...],"lineage":[{"doc","sigs"}...],"next":N,"synced_at":T,"v":"handsal-store/1","witness":null}`, at most MAX_INPUT bytes. `next` is the string `"yes"` or `"no"` (the grammar of WT-02): whether the store holds a valid successor of the pinned document. `acts` is strictly increasing in byte order (sorted, no duplicates) and every item is a string; `lineage` is as in GP-07. There is no `org` member: the hash of the first lineage document is the org (GP-04) and the pin checks compare it, so a store copied from another organization is `lineage_broken`. `witness` is the slot of the relay tip statement and is `null` in this version (WT-03). A reader parses the bytes with the framing of CF-03 and compares them with their own JCS; any other form is `store_malformed`: a CRLF, a missing LF, a second line, unsorted or duplicate acts, an extra or missing key, a `next` that is neither `"yes"` nor `"no"`, another `v`, a non-null `witness`, a non-string act, or more than MAX_INPUT bytes.

Reading. A missing directory or file is `store_missing` (the remedy is `sync`). A directory or file that is a symlink, is not a directory or a regular file, is not owned by the user or is writable by group or others, and a file with a second link, are `store_owner` (these checks, with the user's uid, are made on the store directory by `lstat` and on the store file by `fstat` of the descriptor it opened, and on nothing else: the ancestors of the directory are not walked, which GP-10 does for the pin). Any other read failure is `store_unavailable`. The production pipeline gives the store reason at VR-02 step 6 (f), after `fixture_profile` and before the lineage checks, so the pin reasons come first and steps 1 to 5 answer before a missing store.

`sync`: `handsal-check sync < input.json`, production build only, framed as CF-03. Input: the closed object `{acts, docs, org}`, the snapshot of CF-13 (`acts` may be empty, `docs` is a set of `{doc, sigs}`, `org` a hashstr); any other key, `pin`, `now`, `synced_at`, `tip_statement`, `witness` and `lineage` included, is `malformed_input`. Steps, first failure wins, and nothing is written on a failure:
1. framing and shape: `unparseable_input`, `malformed_input`;
2. the pin and the lineage: VR-02 step 6 (a) to (h) on the in-process probe, the input `org` and the lineage that `docs` gives for the pin, the path from the original to the document whose hash is the pin's `genesis`, each link by `prev`: that document absent from `docs` is `genesis_pin_mismatch`, a missing link is `lineage_broken`, and documents off the path are ignored and not stored; `fixture_profile` for a fixture lineage. This runs before the lock and before any directory is created, so a host with no pin, and a WSL2 host, create nothing;
3. take the lock, one blocking exclusive lock on `<hex64>.lock`, never deleted, then read the old store inside it: absent is the empty set; `store_owner`, `store_malformed` and `store_unavailable` refuse, and an unreadable store is never overwritten;
4. the union of the stored and the input acts, and of the stored and input receipts of each path document;
5. the fold of the union under the input lineage: a state or `fork` continues, every other fold code and `verifier_unavailable` refuse; and the valid descendants of the pinned document in `docs` (each by `genesis-check` of its path over the union), which are open epochs and are not stored;
6. the retained set (CF-14), with the stored lineage closing the epochs before the pin and the valid descendants of the pinned document anchored as open epochs; an act of an epoch off the pinned lineage that is no descendant of the pin is dropped, because the pin moves only forward along one lineage (GP-21);
7. `next`: `"yes"` if the stored lineage ends at the pinned document and the stored `next` is `"yes"`, or if a valid successor of the pinned document was found at step 5, else `"no"`;
8. the retained store over MAX_INPUT is `store_full`;
9. `synced_at` is the clock, read once, here: write a temporary file created exclusively without following links, `fsync` it, `rename` it over the store and `fsync` the directory. An I/O failure is `store_unavailable`. The rename is the point where the new store becomes visible and the directory `fsync` the point where it is durable. A refusal before the rename leaves the old store and removes the temporary file. A refusal at the directory `fsync` after the rename may leave the new store visible, which is safe because every store written holds the retained set of everything the machine has read, and a later `sync` converges. Release the lock.

Output `{"reason":R,"synced_at":T}`. `synced_at` is not null exactly when the store was written and made durable: R is then `ok` (the fold reached a state), `fork`, or `epoch_superseded` when `next` is `"yes"`, never `fork` then (VR-12; the store is written and `check-act` and `enrolled` refuse until a re-pin, VR-12). On a refusal R is the code and `synced_at` is null, including a refusal at the directory `fsync` of step 6, after which the new store may already be in place. A `sync` that adds no act still writes, because `synced_at` says the source was consulted and the fold re-run.

Invariants. The store never loses an item that any command on the machine reads, now or after any re-pin the pin can make: `sync` writes the retained set of the union, so it drops only what no fold from the pin reads (CF-14), whether the input is the whole bag or only new acts, and a string that is no chain item is not stored. The path of every earlier epoch to its change's head stays, so a head on it keeps its evidence (L9 (c)). A forked union is written with `fork`: a fork is a verdict about valid chain acts (FO-24), and storing it makes `check-act` refuse at once instead of answering on the bag from before the fork. The stored lineage is the path the pin accepted at the write, with `sigs` sorted and unique. `synced_at` is set only by `sync`, from its own clock. The lock is held across the read, the union, the fold and the rename, so two syncs both land and the second union holds the first one's acts. Readers take no lock: one rename in one directory gives them the whole old file or the whole new one. A temporary file left by a killed writer is removed under the lock before the new one is created. `sync` answers `unsupported_command` in the conformance build, and no vector covers it or the store reader: they are proven by implementation tests (CF-07), because no store crosses an implementation boundary.

Why: the production input used to ask every caller for state only handsal understands, so three consumers on one machine could hold three bags and disagree about what the chain is. The view now has one owner per machine, one writer and one reader, the move CF-08 made for the pin and the clock. Each store code names one fact with one remedy: `store_missing` (run `sync`), `store_owner` (fix the directory), `store_malformed` (delete the file and sync again), `store_unavailable` (disk), `store_full` (the organization's capacity, L15).

Bound: the store is the user's, so an agent running as the user can withhold acts from `sync`, delete the store, hold its lock or edit `synced_at` (section 14, L10, L11). `sync` itself never rolls the store back past what its readers need.

### CF-10 The server build

### CF-11 `admit`

`handsal-check-server admit < input.json` (server and conformance builds, framed as CF-03) decides whether one item may enter the chain store of an organization. The rule is valid now, never "could matter later": a pending primary, a lone endorsement and a lone `genesis.sign` receipt are never admitted, so pending acts travel to their co-signers outside the chain store. Every admitted item costs Q persons' signatures, so the store grows only as fast as trust changes are made, and a `device.add` that no quorum made adds no kid to any state, so nothing its key signs is admissible (L1). Validity is permanent (section 6), so an admitted item stays valid in every larger store.

Input: the closed object `{acts, docs, item, org}`: the snapshot of CF-13 and `item`, exactly `{act, sigs}` or exactly `{doc, sigs}` (`act` a compact JWS string, `doc` a genesis document, `sigs` a list of strings). Anything else is `malformed_input`. `sigs` are every signature the publisher holds for the item, so the caller encodes no rule.

Store checks, first failure wins: an empty `docs`, or none whose hash is `org` with a null `prev`, is `lineage_broken`, unless the item is that original (registration); every document of `docs` is a valid link from the original (GP-15 order, `genesis-check` of its path), else its code; in the server build a fixture profile anywhere is `fixture_profile`. Acts of the store that are not valid are ignored, as the fold ignores them (FO-06).

Act group, in order: the checks of VR-02 steps 1 to 5 (`jws_shape`, `payload_not_canonical_subset`, `payload_too_large`, `schema`, `method_unknown`), except that after `method_unknown` a method other than the three chain primaries and `chain.resolve` is `not_chain_act` (a verdict, a decision, an endorsement or a `genesis.sign` is not a group's act), then `schema` and `body_schema`; `org_mismatch`; `already_stored` (the receipt id, ACT-12, is that of a node or an applicable resolve of the store, so a valid copy is stored: a low-S twin has the same receipt id, so one JWS is stored per receipt id; a string of `acts` with that receipt id that is no valid act of the store, a garbage twin, does not count, FO-21); `parent_unknown` (the act's parent is not a node of the act tree, FO-26, of an admitted epoch: publish the parent first, nothing is held pending); `epoch_closed` (the parent is a node of an epoch the store holds a successor of: re-pin and publish in the new epoch); `high_s` (the act's own signature, VR-04); `kid_repeated` (two texts of `sigs` that read as endorsements of the act name one kid); `act_not_valid` (not valid against the state at its parent in the store with the signatures given: FO-22, FO-23, FO-07, FO-25 applicability, SG-09 link code, GP-18; a resolve whose candidates or heads are no nodes too; the fold gives no reason for an ignored act, so one code); `store_full`; else `ok`.

Document group, in order: `already_stored` (the document hash is in the store); an original whose hash is not `org`, or a change whose `org` is not `org`, is `lineage_broken`; a change whose `prev` is not in the store is `parent_unknown`; `kid_repeated` (two texts of `sigs` that read as `genesis.sign` receipts of the document name one kid); `genesis-check` of the path ending at this document, with its code (`genesis_head_unknown` when H is no node of the previous epoch's act tree in the store); `store_full`; else `ok`.

Kept signatures (the signatures the retained set of CF-14 keeps). For an act: the endorsements that count (FO-21, FO-08: by a key active and accepted in the state at the parent) and the possession endorsement of the new key. For a document: the `genesis.sign` receipts that count for possession or authority (GP-15, ACT-13). A high-S signature in `sigs` does not qualify (FO-23), a signature by a kid that is no such key is dropped, and nothing else is stored. A kid is named by at most one text of `sigs`: a second text for one kid, a low-S twin included, is `kid_repeated`, answered before any signature is verified. So the verifier is asked at most once per key of the state at the parent (and once for the new key), whatever `sigs` holds, and the cost of one item is bounded by the size of that state, not by the size of the input. The same text twice is one text.

Capacity. Size is the canonical size of CF-13 with the item added. An act group is admitted only while it is at most MAX_INPUT minus 2 times ADMIT_RESERVE, a document group while it is at most MAX_INPUT minus ADMIT_RESERVE; over is `store_full`. A genesis change, the exit of FO-27, is therefore admitted after acts no longer are, and every snapshot a source serves fits `sync` (CF-09) with room for its own keys.

Output `{"reason":R,"store":S}`: S is the item with only the kept signatures (`sigs` sorted) when R is `ok`, else null. A caller writes each string of `store` create-only and reports `already_stored` as success.

Why: admitting what could become valid later lets one member's active key author unlimited distinct pending primaries, each permanent in an append-only store, with no touch from anyone else. Why one signature per kid and one JWS per receipt id: the fold counts a person once (FO-13, GP-15), a second signature by one kid never changes validity, and a store keyed by JWS would hold one act many times. Why a repeated kid is refused and not dropped: choosing among a kid's texts means verifying them all, and a publisher that sends 800 forged texts of one kid would make the server do 800 verifications; refusing the repeat is the only rule under which the verification count is bounded by construction. A caller that holds two signatures of one kid sends one. Why causal order: a pending area is state the server would have to bound and expire. Why `epoch_closed`: an act in a closed epoch is never on a successor path, so a compaction would drop it at once, and a store must not admit what its own retained set would drop. Why `already_stored` needs a valid copy: a store holding a garbage twin, hand built or older, would refuse the valid act for ever.

### CF-12 `pool`

`handsal-check-server pool < input.json` (server and conformance builds, framed as CF-03) answers whether a point is an `agree` key of the organization.

Input: the closed object `{acts, agree, docs, org}`: `agree` is `{x, y}` in the ACT-11 form (a point that is not on the curve is `malformed_input`) and the rest is the snapshot of CF-13. The store checks are those of CF-11. Output `{"in_pool":B,"reason":R}`: `in_pool:true` only with `reason:"ok"`, else false with `not_in_pool` or a store code.

Membership: true iff the point is the `agree` point of a device listed in any document of `docs`, or of any device in the state of any node of the act tree of any admitted epoch, every branch included, losing and forked branches too. A removed device stays in. A `sign` point is not in the pool: no relay operation proves possession of a sign key. The answer means this point was introduced as an `agree` key by the organization at some time on some branch; it does not say active, current or accepted.

Monotone under admission: documents and nodes are permanent (section 6), and FO-07 never re-admits a removed kid, so a `true` for a store is `true` for every store that admission makes larger. A compaction (CF-14) may remove from the pool exactly the points that only dropped nodes held, so a caller that caches `true` clears that cache when it applies a drop. A caller MUST NOT cache `false`.

Why: "active at the tip" needs a pin to choose a lineage and a rule for a fork, and the one use that exists (a mailbox deciding whom to seal to) needs neither.

### CF-13 Sources and the snapshot

A source is anything a machine or a server fetches the chain from. It serves snapshots `{acts, docs, org}`: `acts` an admit-closed subset of the organization's chain bag (all of it or only new groups), `docs` every document the source holds with its receipts, a set of `{doc, sigs}` entries keyed by document hash (entries for one document are one entry, `sigs` united), and `org` a hashstr. Admit-closed means whole groups: an act with every signature stored with it, each admitted by the source's `admit` (CF-11) and kept by its compactions (CF-14). The shape is read by one parser, for `sync` (CF-09), `admit` (CF-11) and `pool` (CF-12): it is verbatim the `sync` input and the store part of the other two. Its size is that of its canonical form: JCS of `{acts, docs, org}`, `acts` sorted and unique, `docs` sorted by document hash, each `sigs` sorted and unique.

A machine with several sources runs one `sync` per source it reaches: CF-09 writes the union of the stored and the input acts, each successful fold advances `synced_at`, and a source that is down never blocks another. A snapshot may be partial but MUST carry every document the source holds, which are few, and whole groups. Publishing is a transport operation whose gate is `admit` on the source's side. The transport is a pipe, `fetch | handsal-check sync`: handsal fetches nothing and holds no address, because a source's URL is transport configuration and not identity. The `tip` operation (WT-03) stays reserved until the relay path is specified, when the snapshot gains a member for it.

Residual: `synced_at` says some source was consulted, not which (VR-12, L10), and a source that withholds a successor document withholds `epoch_superseded` too (L10).

Why: `sync` taking a set of snapshots would merge in one call what several calls already merge, and one unreachable source would fail the call. A second shape for `admit` would be a second copy of the first.

### CF-14 What a store may drop, and `compact`

A store keeps what a reader can read and nothing else. The rule is one function of the fold's own read set, applied by each store's single writer: the relay through `compact` (below), a machine inside `sync` (CF-09). For a store whose documents all pass the store checks of CF-11:
- the epoch of a document D is the acts whose chain reaches D's hash as root (FO-04, GP-07);
- D is **closed** when the store holds a document whose `prev` is D's hash (a successor, sibling successors included), else **open**;
- the **retained set** is: (1) every document of the store with the receipts that count for possession or authority (GP-15, GP-19); (2) of an open epoch, every node of its act tree (FO-26), every branch, losing and forked ones included, and every applicable resolve (FO-25); (3) of a closed epoch, the nodes on the path from its root to the head H of each successor in the store, the union over sibling successors; (4) with every retained primary or resolve A at parent P, every endorsement of A that passes FO-08 and FO-21 against `S_P`, and the possession endorsement of A's new key (FO-07); (5) one copy per receipt id, the smallest valid text in byte order (FO-21, ACT-12).

Everything else is dropped: acts of a closed epoch off every successor path, and the resolves of a closed epoch; strings that are no chain item (not a JWS, schema failures, verdicts, decisions, `genesis.sign` receipts in `acts`, acts of another organization, whether or not their parent is a node of the store); invalid copies, and every valid copy but one per receipt id; endorsements that fail FO-08 or FO-21 against `S_P`; receipts that count for neither possession nor authority; pending primaries, resolves that are not applicable, and acts whose parent is no node.

Theorem: for every pin whose document is open in the store, every answer of `fold`, `check-act`, `enrolled`, `recheck-act`, `head`, `genesis-check` and `sync` is the same over the retained set as over the whole store, and the retained set of the retained set is itself. Each of those answers reads, for every earlier epoch, the path to the H of the change that follows (GP-19 Close, VR-17) and the whole last epoch (FO-24, FO-25), with every signature that a counting, possession or effective-entry rule reads; the retained set holds exactly those items, and each dropped class is one that a rule says is never read (FO-06, FO-15, FO-21, FO-23, GP-19 Path). A machine's store holds its pinned path, so its own pinned epoch is never closed in it and a machine never compacts the epoch it answers from (CF-09).

A pin the store supersedes, a closed epoch, is outside the theorem, and this is what it answers. A machine whose store holds the successor has `next` `"yes"` (CF-09), and `sync` writes `next` `"yes"` in the same write that brings a compacted snapshot, so a machine does not read a compacted closed epoch without being told. `check-act`, `enrolled` and `head` then never answer `ok`, over the retained set or over the whole store: `epoch_superseded` is unconditional at `next` `"yes"` and replaces a `fork` (VR-12). `head` reads no key or device, so it answers `epoch_superseded` with a null head over both stores and never differs between them. The two stores differ only through the tip, which is the end of the path to the successor's H over the retained set and the end of the whole store's accepted chain (or a fork) over the whole store: over the whole store a fork is converted before the key or the device is read, so the answer is `epoch_superseded`; over the retained set a key or a device that lives only on a branch the compaction removed is refused first, `unknown_key` by `check-act` (step 9) and `local_key_unknown` by `enrolled` (step 5), and the `class` of a `check-act` result is the class of the key in that state (null where the state does not hold it). Each is a refusal in both stores. `fold` and `recheck-act` read no `next` and can differ there: `fold` answers a state where the whole store answered `fork`, or a state with another tip (the end of the path to H, not the end of the whole store's accepted chain); `recheck-act` answers `ok` for a head on the path to the successor's H where the whole store answered `stale_view` (the head was past a fork parent); a head on the whole store's accepted chain that is on no retained path goes the other way, `ok` to `stale_view`. Only the second direction fails closed, and neither is an authorization: no `ok` is new for a head off every successor path, and every command that authorizes something refuses with `epoch_superseded` or an earlier refusal. Both are the answers the machine gives after its re-pin to that successor, whose quorum chose that H (GP-19), and a person's re-pin is the remedy for both (GP-12).

`compact`: `handsal-check-server compact < input.json` (server and conformance builds, framed as CF-03) takes exactly the snapshot of CF-13 `{acts, docs, org}`, anything else is `malformed_input`. Its store checks are those of CF-11 without the registration case: no original is `lineage_broken`, every document must be a valid link or its GP-15 code is the answer, a fixture profile in the server build is `fixture_profile`, and a verifier that cannot answer is `verifier_unavailable`. Output `{"drop":{"acts":A,"sigs":G},"reason":"ok"}`: A is every string of `acts` that the retained set of the whole store does not hold, G every receipt text of a document's `sigs` that it does not hold, both sorted and unique and possibly empty; on a refusal `{"drop":null,"reason":R}`. It writes nothing: the caller deletes what `drop` lists. Any subset of `drop` leaves a store whose answers equal the whole store's for every pin the store does not supersede; compacting the result drops nothing; and `admit` on the compacted store answers as on the whole store, except a change whose H was dropped, which is `genesis_head_unknown` (CF-11).

Operator rules: a store MUST serialize document admission with computing and applying a drop list: no document is admitted from the moment the list is computed until its application is complete. A document admitted in between has a head that the list, computed without it, may drop (the head is on no successor path), and applying the list then deletes the head path of an admitted document, so every later call on it is `genesis_head_unknown`. Acts need no such lock: an act in a closed epoch is refused `epoch_closed`, and an act in an open epoch is never listed. A store SHOULD compact an epoch only after it has been closed for at least the `max_view_age_s` of its policy, unless an act is refused `store_full`. A caller that caches `pool` answers clears its cached `true` after applying a drop (CF-12).

Why a command and not a rule inside the relay: the store's operator holds bytes and never a rule, the same reason as CF-11. Why the retained set and not "what `admit` would now refuse": validity is permanent (section 6), so that set is empty and frees nothing. Why every valid endorsement is kept and none is chosen: an `overrides` entry names endorsements by receipt id (FO-25), so a choice could make an effective entry ineffective and change a fork answer. Why every sibling successor is kept: which one counts is a person's re-pin (GP-12, WT-10), and a store that dropped one would choose for them.
